1 · The kid's app — v1 (Sprout & Sapling)

The kid side is the product. Sprout is a toy; Sapling is a storybook town. Nothing here may feel like a bank.

The Jar

Sproutbuilt
Job
The whole app for a 4-year-old. One object, two actions, zero navigation. Money is coins in a jar you can almost touch.
Layout
Giant count in the money font (8), one line of words under it, the jar filling ~60% of the screen, two river-stone buttons. Nothing else. The sprout on the lid gains a leaf at savings milestones.
Copy
Mints in your jar · You earned 5 Mints! — five words or fewer, always.
Motion
Sprout sways every ~20s (idle life). Add: coin drops from above, bounces twice, settles; +1 floats up; clink sound, pitch rising per earn in a session.
States
Empty jar shows one seed at the bottom: Do a job. Grow a Mint. Spend opens a picture-only store sheet (no reading required).
Your call
  • Jar style: mason jar (current), honey pot, or fishbowl?
  • Should the count be spoken aloud on tap for pre-readers?
  • Background: plain cream (current) or the meadow scene behind the jar?

Kid Home

Saplingbuilt
Job
The town square. Answers three kid questions in one glance: how much do I have, how close is my goal, what can I do to earn more.
Layout
Scenic hero with curved bottom (mountains, pines, growing mint sprig), balance floating on it. Three pastel stat blocks (+18 this week / saved up / jobs open). Berry goal card with Growth Ring around a photo of the actual thing. Jobs preview. Store shelf preview. Dark evergreen pill nav: Home · Jobs · Store · Bank.
Copy
Hi, Maya · 12 more Mints to your goal. Kid is always "you". No exclamation points on anything negative.
Motion
Ring ticks forward when balance changes. Scene parallaxes very slightly on scroll (fade-only under reduced motion).
Your call
  • Scene: one fixed valley, or does it change with seasons / time of day?
  • Should the scene visibly grow with total savings (more trees, taller sprig) — the home screen as garden?
  • Stat blocks: keep three, or two bigger ones (this week + goal)?

Jobs Board — kid

Saplingv1
Job
Where money is earned. Chores the parent posted, plus the kid's own pitches, on one board.
Layout
Quest cards: left color spine (evergreen = chore, berry = your pitch), title, reward in gold, due chip. One tap flips the card to detail: what to do, then a single big button I did it → status becomes "waiting for Mom/Dad". A floating dashed "+" card at top: Pitch a job.
Copy
Water the garden · 8 Mints · after submit: Nice. Waiting for Dad to check.
Motion
Card flip on open. On approval (push arrives): the card celebrates — spine flashes gold, coins hop to the nav's Bank icon.
States
Empty board: No jobs right now. Pitch one? — the empty state sells the pitch mechanic.
Your call
  • Recurring chores: show every week as a fresh card, or a persistent card with a weekly checkmark streak?
  • Do completed jobs stay visible (a "done pile" that grows, satisfying) or vanish?

Pitch a Job — the soul

Saplingv1
Job
The most important behavior we teach: name your work, name your price, negotiate. This screen must look unlike everything else — dashed border, lightbulb, special.
Layout
Two fields, huge: what will you do (text), what is it worth (number stepper with the leaf coin). One button: Send my pitch. After sending, the pitch card lives on the board with a "waiting" state. If the parent counters: the card opens showing both numbers — You asked 25. Dad offers 15. — with Deal and No thanks. Full history stays on the card forever (their first negotiation, preserved).
Copy
Placeholder text does the teaching: Wash the car, rake the leaves, read to your sister…
Motion
Send: the card folds into a paper plane and flies to the top of the board. Deal: both numbers merge into one, ring ticks.
Your call
  • Price input: free number, or a slider with the family's typical range hinted?
  • Paper-plane send animation — charming or too cute? It sets the tone for the whole mechanic.
  • Should a declined pitch offer a gentle retry prompt (Try a smaller job?) or stay silent?

House Store

Saplingv1
Job
Where Mints become real life. Family-priced privileges on drawn shelves — it should read as a tiny storefront, not a list.
Layout
Items sit on 3px evergreen shelves with bracket feet. Each: emoji, name, price in gold, Buy. Affordable items glow softly; unaffordable ones stay full-color (never grayed out — aspiration, not punishment) with the gap shown.
Copy
Insufficient funds is math, never shame: You have 40. This costs 75. 35 to go. with a jump to the jobs board.
Motion
Buy: the item card flips to a ticket — a real-looking perforated stub the kid shows the parent. Ticket sits in a "my tickets" pocket until the parent marks it done.
Your call
  • Ticket look: carnival stub, cinema ticket, or receipt? This becomes a collectible.
  • Do spent tickets archive into a scrapbook (memory value) or disappear?

The Bank

Saplingv1
Job
Two-pocket money made physical: Wallet (spend) and Savings (grow), later Vault and Education. Teaches the single most valuable habit: moving money on purpose.
Layout
Each account is a vessel: wallet = open pouch, savings = jar with a sprout, vault = strongbox, education = graduation-cap jar. Balances in the money font. Tap-and-drag (or tap-tap) between vessels opens the transfer sheet: amount stepper, one confirm.
Copy
Move 10 to Savings? · after: Savings grew.
Motion
The signature: coins arc from vessel to vessel; the receiving Growth Ring ticks forward. Interest day (below) happens here.
Your call
  • Vessel metaphors above — right set? (This is the app's second-most-drawn illustration set.)
  • Drag-to-move vs tap-tap: drag is delightful but harder at 6; ship both?

Interest Day

Saplingv1
Job
The weekly proof that saved money grows by itself. The moment must feel like receiving mail, not reading a statement.
Layout
Not a screen — an event on the Bank screen. A small envelope slides in, opens; coins hop into Savings; the ring ticks. A one-line note: Your savings grew 2 Mints while you slept.
Motion
Envelope slide + soft chime (respects mute). Under reduced motion: fade in the note.
Your call
  • Sunday morning or Monday after school — when does interest feel best?
  • Envelope sender: "The Bank", the family name, or the sprout itself?

Goals

Saplingv1
Job
Wanting something specific is the engine of saving. A goal is a photo of the real thing with a ring growing around it.
Layout
Create: camera/photo picker, name, price. Detail: the photo in a circle, Growth Ring filling clockwise, 12 more Mints to your goal., an Add Mints button, and a small forecast line: About 3 more weeks at your pace.
Motion
Ring fill on every deposit. At 100%: the ring blooms — leaves unfurl around the photo — then converts to a Buy ticket.
Your call
  • One goal at a time (focus) or up to three (siblings will compare)?
  • The pace forecast — motivating or pressure? Include at Sapling or hold for Grove?

The Vault

Saplingv1
Job
Delayed gratification with teeth: lock Mints for 30/60/90 days at better rates. Early withdrawal isn't penalized — it's impossible. Simpler to teach.
Layout
Three lock choices as chunky physical switches with rates in gold. Locked state: strongbox with a ring counting down the days, unlock date written like a birthday: Opens March 4.
Motion
Locking: the lid shuts with a satisfying clunk. Unlock day: the box springs open — a genuine celebration, coins + chime.
Your call
  • Do we show accrued interest live inside the locked box, or reveal it only at opening (surprise value)?

Education Vault

Sapling+v1
Job
The long jar. Kid deposits are matched by real-world action: the parent gets a 529 prompt and, when they confirm, bonus Mints mint on the kid's side.
Layout
Kid side: a jar that never opens, tallest ring in the app, copy about the future self. Parent side (in their app): deep-link to CollegeAdvantage (or their own link) + Match settled confirmation.
Copy
This jar is for grown-up you. It only ever grows.
Your call
  • Match ratio default (1:1? 2:1?) and whether the kid sees that the match came from a real account.

Activity

Sapling+v1
Job
The ledger, kid-readable. Every Mint's story — earned, moved, spent, grown — because the append-only ledger is the product's honesty made visible.
Layout
A vertical vine. Each transaction is a leaf on it: icon by kind (job, store, interest, move), plain sentence, amount signed and colored with arrows. Grouped by week: This week you grew 23 Mints.
Your call
  • Vine metaphor vs plain grouped list — how literal should the growth language get here?

Era Graduation

every erav1 mechanism
Job
The parent decides readiness; the kid experiences a rite. The app visibly grows up in the kid's hands — this is the brand promise performed.
Layout
Full-screen takeover on next open. Three beats: (1) the old era's leaf grows into the new one, morphing on the coin; (2) You're ready for more. + two lines about what unlocks; (3) the UI itself transitions — corners sharpen, colors shift — live, in front of them.
Motion
The token transition is the animation: radius, palette, and type interpolate over ~2s. Sound: the era chime (one of the two allowed sounds after Sapling).
Your call
  • Can the kid replay the ceremony from settings, or is it once-only (scarcity = memory)?
  • Should the parent get a recording/screenshot moment auto-offered?

2 · The kid's app — later eras

Grove is v2 ("your kid unlocked credit" is the marketing beat). Canopy is v3. Harvest ships in v1's code, unreachable — the north star costs one screen.

Money HQ

Grovev2
Job
The home screen becomes a command center. Dark by default — presented as a rite of passage, not a toggle. First boot: the screen "powers on": Welcome to Money HQ. Kids will screenshot it.
Layout
Dense on purpose: net worth ticker (counts digit by digit, never fades), fund sparklines, credit dial, bills due strip, jobs. Data becomes decoration. Cards separate by tone, not shadow.
Copy
Straight facts, light edge: Rocket Fund fell 8%. That is what high risk means.
Your call
  • How dangerous should Grove feel? Current spec is "strategy game"; the alternative is "trading terminal". One notch matters at 11.

Funds · Loans · Credit · Bills

Grovev2
Job
Four lessons: volatility (three fictional funds, server-driven weekly walk — the Rocket Fund lesson happens on its own), debt (loan term sheets, serious on purpose; payoff is one of two sound moments), reputation (family credit score 600 start, +12 on-time / −18 missed, needle sweeps on change), and obligations (Life Mode rent/internet/phone — rent secretly routes to the kid's savings for the graduation reveal).
Layout
Funds: sparkline cards + buy/sell in whole Mints. Loans: label column / value column / two buttons. Credit: half-arc dial owns violet. Bills: due-date strip with autopay toggle.
Your call
  • Fund names/personalities (Rocket / Steady / Seed?) — you get to name the kid's first market.
  • Missed-payment shake + red: harsh enough to sting once, or add a "grace day"?

Canopy: Paycheck · Curve · Shadow Portfolio

Canopyv3
Job
Clean fintech a 16-year-old would choose. Paycheck screen shows gross→streams (family tax, vacation, charity) as a tappable flow — the era's one rich visual. Compound screen: a projection curve you scrub with a thumb, watching the age-45 number swing. Shadow portfolio: real tickers, live prices, ghost icon — real market, zero risk, side by side with the real custodial account when parents enable it.

3 · The parent's app

Same design system, calmer registers. The metric that rules every parent screen: total daily time under 60 seconds.

Download → working economy in under 3 minutes, timed. Four steps, every default pre-filled: name the economy + currency → add kids (era suggested from birth year, editable; weekly base income) → stock the store from templates → confirm.
Copy

  • Currency naming: offer suggestions (Acorns, Stars, Berries) or leave it blank-slate?

Approvals Inbox — the 10-second screen

parentv1
Job
The screen that decides whether parents stay. Every pending thing — job done, pitch made, ticket to fulfill — in one list, each resolvable in one tap. Target: under 10 seconds a day.
Layout
Cards sorted oldest-first. Each: kid avatar, one plain sentence (Maya says she watered the garden · 8), and inline actions — Approve / Not yet, Accept / Counter / Decline, Done. Counter expands a number field in place; nothing navigates away. Empty state is the reward: All caught up. with a tiny meadow.
Motion
Approve: the card folds away and the next slides up — inbox-zero momentum. A subtle running count: 3 → 2 → 1 → 🌱.
Your call
  • Batch-approve ("approve all chores") — huge time-saver, but does it cheapen the kid's moment?
  • Should declining always require a reason the kid sees (teaches), or is silence okay?

Respond to a Pitch

parentv1
Job
The other half of the soul. The parent sees the kid's own words and price, then: Accept, Counter (number + optional note), or Decline (reason required — spec: teaching moment).
Layout
The kid's pitch shown as they wrote it — same dashed card the kid sees, building shared language. Three buttons, counter inline.
Copy
Counter helper text nudges pedagogy: A counter teaches more than a no.
Your call
  • Decline reasons: free text, or a picker (too big a job / not this week / let's talk) that keeps it kind?

4 · Cross-cutting rules (every screen answers to these)

  • Progress is never a bar. Rings, like tree rings — goals, streaks, vault countdowns, tenure.
  • Numbers never age. Every Mint amount in Spline Sans Mono, tabular, in every era, forever.
  • Losses look like losses, once. Red + arrow + one 200ms shake. Never gray-out, never shame words, no exclamation points on bad news.
  • Color never carries meaning alone — arrows and signs always ride along. 4.5:1 contrast floor; gold text on light is always #8A6A1B.
  • Touch floors: 64px Sprout → 48px Sapling → 44px after. Reduced motion gets fade-only everywhere.
  • Sound is scarce: coin clink + interest chime in the young eras; from Grove, only loan payoff and graduation. Mute always wins.
  • Emoji end at Sapling. Illustration ends at Sapling (Canopy keeps at most one hidden sprig — your call above).
  • No mascot. The jar, the coin, and the plant are the characters.
  • The two standing checks: parent's daily time under 60 seconds; and the one-sentence test — logo removed, someone still says "that's the money app that grows up with your kid."
